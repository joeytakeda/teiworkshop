            <p>
               <seg type="dropCap">I</seg> Have obſerved, that a Reader ſeldom peruſes
            <lb/>a Book with Pleaſure ’till he knows whether
            <lb/>the Writer of it be a black or a fair Man, of
            <lb/>a mild or cholerick Diſpoſition, Married or a
            <lb/>Batchelor, with other Particulars of the like
            <lb/>nature, that conduce very much to the right Un<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>derſtanding of an Author. To gratify this Curio
            <lb/>ſity, which is ſo natural to a Reader, I deſign this
            <lb/>Paper, and my next, as Prefatory Diſcourſes to my
            <lb/>following Writings, and ſhall give ſome Account
            <lb/>in them of the ſeveral perſons that are engaged in
            <lb/>this Work. As the chief Trouble of Compiling,
            <lb/>Digeſting<supplied>,</supplied> and Correcting will fall to my Share, I
            <lb/>muſt do my ſelf the Juſtice to open the Work
            <lb/>with my own Hiſtory.</p>
            <p>I was born to a ſmall Hereditary Eſtate, which
               <lb/>I find, by the Writings of the Family, was bounded
               <lb/>by the ſame Hedges and Ditches in <persName ref="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror">
                  <hi rendition="#italic">William</hi> the 
                  <lb/>Conqueror</persName>’s Time that it is at preſent, and has
               <lb/>been delivered down from Father to Son whole 
               <lb/>and entire, without the Loſs or Acquiſition of a 
               <lb/>ſingle Field or Meadow, during the Space of ſix
               <lb/>hundred Years. There goes a Story in the Family,
               <lb/>that when my Mother was gone with Child of me
               <lb/>about three Months, ſhe dreamt that ſhe was brought
               <lb/>to Bed of a Judge: Whether this might proceed
               <lb/>from a Law-Suit which was then depending in
               <lb/>the Family, or my Father’s being a Juſtice of
               <lb/>the Peace, I cannot determine; for I am not ſo
               <lb/>vain as to think it preſaged any Dignity that I ſhould
               <lb/>arrive at in my future Life, though that was the
               <lb/>Interpretation which the Neighbourhood put up<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>on it. The Gravity of my Behaviour at my very firſt
               <lb/>Appearance in the World, and all the Time that I
               <lb/>ſucked, ſeemed to favour my Mothers Dream:
               <lb/>For, as ſhe has often told me, I threw away my
               <lb/>Rattle before I was two Months old, and would
               <lb/>not make uſe of my Coral ’till they had taken
               <lb/>away the Bells from it.</p>
            <p>As for the reſt of my Infancy, there being no<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>thing in it remarkable, I ſhall paſs it over in Silence.
               <lb/>I find, that, during my Nonage, I had the Reputa<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>tion of a very ſullen Youth, but was always a
               <lb/>Favourite of my School-Maſter, who uſed to ſay,
               <lb/>
               <q rendition="#italic">that my parts were ſolid and would wear well</q>. I
               <lb/>had not been long at the Univerſity, before I di<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <cb/>
               <lb/>ſtinguiſhed myſelf by a moſt profound Silence: For
               <lb/>during the Space of eight Years, excepting in
               <lb/>the publick Exerciſes of the College, I ſcarce ut<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>tered the Quantity of an hundred Words; and in<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>deed do not remember that I ever ſpoke three Sen<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>tences together in my whole Life. Whilſt I was
               <lb/>in this Learned Body<supplied>,</supplied> I applied my ſelf with ſo
               <lb/>much Diligence to my Studies, that there are very
               <lb/>few celebrated Books, either in the Learned or the
               <lb/>Modern Tongues, which I am not acquainted
               <lb/>with.
            </p>
            <p>Upon the Death of my Father I was reſolved
               <lb/>to travel into Foreign Countries, and therefore
               <lb/>left the Univerſity, with the Character of an odd
               <lb/>unaccountable Fellow, that had a great deal of
               <lb/>Learning, if I would but ſhow it. An inſatiable
               <lb/>Thirſt after Knowledge carried me into all the
               <lb/>Countries of <placeName rendition="#italic">Europe</placeName>, where there was any thing
               <lb/>new or ſtrange to be ſeen; nay, to ſuch a Degree
               <lb/>was my curioſity raiſed, that having read the Con<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>troverſies of ſome great Men concerning the An<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>tiquities of <placeName rendition="#italic">Egypt</placeName>, I made a Voyage to <placeName rendition="#italic">Grand
                  <lb/>Cairo</placeName>, on purpoſe to take the Meaſure of a Pyra<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>mid; and as ſoon as I had ſet my ſelf right in that
               <lb/>Particular, returned to my Native Country with 
               <lb/>great Satisfaction.</p>
            <p>I have pasſed my latter Years in this City, where
               <lb/>I am frequently ſeen in moſt publick Places, tho’
               <lb/>there are not above half a dozen of my ſelect
               <lb/>Friends that know me; of whom my next Paper
               <lb/>ſhall give a more particular Account. There is
               <lb/>no Place of Publick Reſort,<!--Note the shift in capitalization here--> wherein I do not often
               <lb/>make my Appearance; ſometimes I am ſeen thruſt<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>ing my Head into a Round of Politicians at <placeName ref="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will%27s_Coffee_House">Will</placeName>’s,
               <lb/>and liſt<supplied>e</supplied>ning with great Attention to the Narratives that
               <lb/>are made in thoſe little Circular Audiences. Some<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>times I ſmoak a Pipe at <placeName rendition="#italic">Child</placeName>’s; and, whilſt I ſeem
               <lb/>attentive to nothing but the <hi rendition="#italic">Poſt-Man</hi>, over-hear
               <lb/>the Converſation of every Table in the Room. I
               <lb/>appear on <hi rendition="#italic">Sunday</hi> nights at <placeName ref="https://londontaverns.co.uk/StJamescoffeehouse.shtml">St. <hi rendition="#italic">James</hi>'s Coffee<pc force="strong">-</pc>
                  <lb/>Houſe</placeName>, and ſometimes join the little Committee
               <lb/>of Politicks in the Inner-Room, as one who comes
               <lb/>there to hear and improve. My Face is likewiſe
               <lb/>very well known at the <placeName rendition="#italic">Grecian</placeName>, the <placeName rendition="#italic">Cocoa-Tree</placeName>,
               <lb/>and in the Theaters both of <placeName rendition="#italic">Drury Lane</placeName> and the
               <lb/>
               <placeName rendition="#italic">Hay-Market</placeName>. I have been taken for a Merchant
               <fw rendition="#text-right">upon</fw>
               <pb/>
               <cb/>
               <lb/>upon the <placeName rendition="#italic">Exchange</placeName> for above theſe ten Years, and
               <lb/>ſometimes paſs for a Jew in the Aſſembly of Stock-
               <lb/>Jobbers at <placeName rendition="#italic">Jonathan</placeName>’s. In ſhort, where-ever I ſee
               <lb/>a Cluſter of People I always mix with them, tho’
               <lb/>I never open my Lips but in my own Club.</p>
            <p>Thus I live in the World, rather as a Spectator
               <lb/>of Mankind, than as one of the Species; by
               <lb/>which means I have made my ſelf a Speculative
               <lb/>Stateſman, Soldier, Merchant<supplied>,</supplied> and Artizan, with<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>out ever medling with any Practical Part in Life.
               <lb/>I am very well verſed in the Theory of an Huſ<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>band, or a Father, and can diſcern the Errors in
               <lb/>the O<choice>
                  <sic>c</sic>
                  <corr>e</corr>
               </choice>conomy, Buſineſs, and Diverſion of others,
               <lb/>better than thoſe who are engaged in them;
               <lb/>as Standers-by diſcover Blots, which are apt to
               <lb/>eſcape thoſe who are in the Game. I never eſpou<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>ſed any Party with Violence, and am reſolved to
               <lb/>obſerve an exact Neutrality between the Whigs and
               <lb/>Tories, unleſs I ſhall be forc’d to declare my ſelf
               <lb/>by the Hoſtilities of either ſide. In ſhort, I have
               <lb/>acted in all the parts of my Life as a Looker-
               <lb/>on, which is the Character I intend to preſerve in
               <lb/>this Paper.</p>
            <p>I have given the Reader juſt ſo much of my
               <lb/>Hiſtory and Character, as to let him ſee I am not
               <lb/>altogether unqualified for the Buſinesſ I have un<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>dertaken. As for other Particulars in my Life and
               <lb/>Adventures, I ſhall inſert them in following Pa<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>pers, as I ſhall ſee occaſion. In the mean time,
               <lb/>when I conſider how much I have ſeen, read<supplied>,</supplied> and
               <lb/>heard, I begin to blame my own Taciturnity;
               <lb/>and ſince I have neither Time nor Inclination to
               <lb/>communicate the Fulnesſ of my Heart in Speech,
               <lb/>I am reſolved to do it in Writing; and to Print
               <lb/>my ſelf out, if posſible, before I Die. I have been
               <lb/>often told by my Friends that it is Pity ſo ma<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>ny uſeful Diſcoveries which I have made, ſhould
               <lb/>be in the posſesſion of a Silent Man. For this
               <lb/>Reaſon therefore, I ſhall publiſh a Sheet full of
               <lb/>Thoughts every Morning, for the Benefit of my
               <lb/>Contemporaries; and if I can any way contribute
               <lb/>to the Diverſion or Improvement of the Country
               <lb/>in which I live, I ſhall leave it, when I am ſum<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>moned out of it, with the ſecret Satisfaction of
               <lb/>thinking that I have not Lived in vain.</p>
            <p>There are three very material Points which I
               <lb/>have not ſpoken to in this Paper, and which, for
               <lb/>ſeveral important Reaſons, I muſt keep to my
               <lb/>ſelf, at leaſt for ſome Time: I mean, an Account
               <lb/>of my Name, my Age, and my Lodgings. I muſt
               <lb/>confeſs I would gratify my Reader in any thing
               <lb/>that is reaſonable; but as for theſe three Particu<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>lars, though I am ſenſible they might tend very
               <lb/>much to the Embelliſhment of my Paper, I can<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>not yet come to a Reſolution of communicating
               <lb/>them to the Publick. They would indeed draw
               <lb/>me out of that Obſcurity which I have enjoy’d
               <lb/>for many Years, and expoſe me in Publick Places
               <lb/>to ſeveral Salutes and Civilities, which have been
               <lb/>always very diſagreeable to me; for the greateſt
               <lb/>Pains I can ſuffer, are the being talked to, and be<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>ing ſtared at. It is for this Reaſon likewiſe, that
               <lb/>I keep my Complexion and Dreſs, as very
               <lb/>great Secrets; tho’ it is not imposſible, but I may
               <lb/>make Diſcoveries of both in the Progreſs of the
               <lb/>Work I have undertaken.</p>
            <p>After having been thus particular upon my ſelf,
               <lb/>I ſhall in to-Morrow’s Paper give an Account of
               <lb/>thoſe Gentlemen who are concerned with me in
               <lb/>this Work. For, as I have before intimated, a
               <lb/>Plan of it is laid and concerted (as all other Mat<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>ters of Importance are) in a Club. However, as
               <lb/>my Friends have engaged me to ſtand in the Front,
               <lb/>thoſe who have a mind to correſpond with me, 
               <cb/>
               <lb/>may direct their Letters <q rendition="#italic">To the Spectator</q>, at Mr.
               <lb/>
               <persName rendition="#italic">Buckleys</persName>, in <placeName rendition="#italic">Little Britain</placeName>. For I muſt further
               <lb/>acquaint the Reader, that tho’ our Club meets on<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>ly on <hi rendition="#italic">Tueſdays</hi> and <hi rendition="#italic">Thurſdays</hi>, we have appointed
               <lb/>a Committee to ſit every Night, for the Inſpect<pc force="weak">-</pc>
               <lb/>ion of all ſuch Papers as may contribute to the
               <lb/>Advancement of the Public Weal.</p>
